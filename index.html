What the first 90 days looks like

Most operations problems in manufacturing SMEs fall into four categories: production planning gaps, supplier relationship failures, process inefficiency, and team accountability issues. I've worked across all four — in different combinations, at different business sizes, across manufacturing and commercial environments.

Here's how I approach a new role:

01
Days 1–30

Operational Diagnosis

Before recommending anything, I audit what's actually happening. Not what the reports say. What the floor shows.

  • Map current production workflows end to end
  • Identify where targets are missed and why — root cause, not symptom
  • Assess supplier relationships: reliability, lead times, risk concentration
  • Review inventory management: turnover, dead stock, reorder logic
  • Talk to the people actually doing the work

Output: A clear-eyed picture of where time and money are leaking, prioritised by impact.

02
Days 31–60

Early Improvement

Early results that demonstrate I can solve problems, not just identify them.

  • Introduce or improve production planning protocols
  • Address the highest-impact supplier or procurement issue from the diagnosis
  • Introduce or tighten the KPI framework the team is accountable to
  • Remove bottlenecks slowing output without requiring capital investment

Output: Measurable movement in at least one key metric — OTD, downtime, or procurement cost.

03
Days 61–90

Systems

Build the systems so the business stops depending on specific people to function.

  • Document processes so they survive staff turnover
  • Build supplier performance frameworks that reduce reliance on informal relationships
  • Create reporting rhythms that give the MD visibility without requiring daily involvement
  • Develop the team so operations runs without escalation

Output: Operations that don't require daily MD involvement. A team that owns its targets.

What makes this combination unusual

There are three types of candidates applying for operations roles in UK manufacturing right now.

The Linear Ops Manager

10 years in one sector. Deep domain knowledge. No commercial exposure. Strong in a corporate environment. In an SME where every operational decision has a financial consequence, the gap shows quickly.

The Recent MSc Graduate

Strong academic foundation. Limited operational accountability. Has supported projects. Hasn't owned one end to end.

The Six Sigma Specialist

Process-focused. Excellent where methodology can be followed. Often struggles in resource-constrained SMEs where judgment matters more than framework.

Then there's a less common fourth path:

An engineer who took operations ownership seriously, gained deliberate commercial exposure, and then upgraded academically to understand the business logic behind the shop floor.
